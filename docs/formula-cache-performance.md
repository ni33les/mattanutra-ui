## Changes

- Complete the immutable catalogue/contract/instructions message before the separate customer-context message. Preserve every input fact, previous formula, feedback and chat entry.
- Supply xAI's optional `x-grok-conv-id` routing header. Its opaque SHA-256 identity includes the immutable messages, model, reasoning setting and response schema. Customer answers, handles and previous results never enter the key. This routes provider prompt work; it does not cache customer answers or share conversation state.
- Give the three ingredient explanation fields distinct roles: short benefit, assessment connection and dose/status reason. Retain all output fields and full linked cautions. Length guidance is advisory; nothing is truncated after generation.
- Advance the internal formula prompt to `v3-cached-concise`. All callers without a routing hint retain their existing transport. The provider schema, semantic validation and bounded retries remain unchanged.

The header and complete-prefix design follow [xAI cache routing](https://docs.x.ai/developers/advanced-api-usage/prompt-caching/maximizing-cache-hits) and [prefix caching](https://docs.x.ai/developers/advanced-api-usage/prompt-caching/how-it-works). Cache hits remain provider-controlled.

All four returned ten ingredients and passed existing validation on the first response. The candidate medium samples used 37–43% fewer characters across `rationale`, `decision` and `whyThisIsForYou`. Fresh model outputs differed in doses and ordering; these samples do **not** establish deterministic formulation or equivalent clinical quality. No stored formula is regenerated by this change.

Cache reuse across different customers was **not demonstrated**: a second synthetic customer took 46.14 s and reported zero cached tokens. Thai and Chinese requests also cached only 384 tokens. A separate two-call diagnostic placing static facts in the system message did not improve shared-prefix hits; its deliberately truncated output is not counted as a successful formula. That experimental layout was not adopted. Only the identical repeat demonstrated substantial cache reuse. There is no claimed cache speed-up for new customers or guaranteed overall speed-up.

Thai and Chinese candidate-medium outputs passed first-response validation in 59.44 s and 44.09 s. Their benefit/assessment/decision prose was inspected in the requested languages; canonical names and machine-readable dose units remained intact. There is no same-locale baseline speed claim for these samples.

## Low versus medium reasoning

Two paired synthetic profiles were compared, without changing runtime configuration:

| Profile | Medium | Low | Quality observations |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Ordinary, statin context | 38.74 s | 30.48 s | Both valid; low substituted B6 for L-carnitine and changed prioritisation. |
| Pregnancy, apixaban, kidney concerns, unknown supplements | 69.56 s | 44.66 s | Both valid; all four contexts had linked cautions. Medium returned 12 ingredients, low 10, with materially different proposed doses. |

These are exploratory probes, not clinical approval. The complex outputs also contained clinician-approval/review-only wording despite the existing advisory instructions. Structural validation does not prove those instructions were followed semantically. This limitation is preserved in evidence; no new purchase gate is implemented. Lower reasoning is **not recommended for rollout on this evidence**. Medium remains the default, and no live setting was changed.
